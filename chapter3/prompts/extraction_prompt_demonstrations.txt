Ontology:
---------
NODE TYPES (6 Total):
1) Company:
   - Entities such as corporations, subsidiaries, parent companies, or government organizations that manage assets.
   - Do NOT extract brand names or stock/index names.
2) Location:
   - Recognizable cities, states, provinces, countries, or distinct regions.
   - Exclude index groupings or names tied to brands.
3) Material:
   - Raw materials, metals, minerals, chemicals, ores, or basic compounds (e.g., "lithium", "gold", "tetraethyllead").
   - Include synonyms such as "raw metals" or "ores" when clearly used.
4) Product:
   - Finished manufactured or refined items sold as discrete goods (including branded items).
   - Examples include general categories (e.g., "lithium batteries"), specific models, engine components, or cars.
5) Person:
   - Clearly identified individuals.
6) Mine:
   - Mining sites, projects, or extraction facilities; include synonyms like "pit", "field", "operation", or "project" when used in context.

RELATIONSHIP TYPES (Use EXACT Labels):
1) produces:
   - Domain: Company → (Material or Product)
   - Extract only when the text explicitly states or strongly implies that production, manufacturing, or extraction is occurring.
   - In addition to earlier synonyms ("manufactures", "builds", "develops", "extracts"), also consider phrases like "is the producer of", "creates", "is manufacturing".
2) suppliesTo:
   - Domain: Company → Company
   - Extract only when the text clearly indicates that one company supplies, delivers, or provides to another.
3) locatedIn:
   - Domain: (Company or Mine) → Location
   - Extract only when the text explicitly shows a headquarters, a main operational site, or a registration location by using phrases like "based in", "headquartered in", "located in", "operates from", or "situated in".
4) owns:
   - Domain: Company → (Company or Mine)
   - Extract only when there is clear evidence of acquisition or ownership using terms such as "acquired", "bought", "purchased", "holds a stake in", "controls", or "took over".

Extraction Guidelines & Best Practices:
-----------------------------------------
1. Read the text thoroughly (at least three passes) to capture every potential node and relationship.
2. For each candidate node, extract only if there is clear, unambiguous evidence.

3. For relationships, extract only if the connection is explicitly established by the text. Do not invent relationships.
   - For produces, own, and locatedIn, check for all provided synonyms and variants.
4. Avoid false positives:
   - Do NOT extract ambiguous or index-related terms (e.g., "S&P 500").
   - Do NOT create relationships without clear textual evidence.
5. Re-read the text after your initial extraction to ensure nothing is missed.

Lithium hydroxide is an inorganic compound with the formula LiOH. It can exist as anhydrous or hydrated, and both forms are white hygroscopic solids. They are soluble in water and slightly soluble in ethanol. Both are available commercially. While classified as a strong base, lithium hydroxide is the weakest known alkali metal hydroxide.


== Production ==
The preferred feedstock is hard-rock spodumene, where the lithium content is expressed as % lithium oxide.


=== Lithium carbonate route ===
Lithium hydroxide is often produced industrially from lithium carbonate in a metathesis reaction with calcium hydroxide: 

Li2CO3 + Ca(OH)2 → 2 LiOH + CaCO3
The initially produced hydrate is dehydrated by heating under vacuum up to 180 °C.


=== Lithium sulfate route ===
An alternative route involves the intermediacy of lithium sulfate:

α-spodumene → β-spodumene
β-spodumene + CaO → Li2O + ...
Li2O + H2SO4 → Li2SO4 + H2O
Li2SO4 + 2 NaOH → Na2SO4 + 2 LiOH
The main by-products are gypsum and sodium sulphate, which have some market value.

== Applications ==


=== Lithium-ion batteries ===
Lithium hydroxide is mainly consumed in the production of cathode materials for lithium-ion batteries such as lithium cobalt oxide (LiCoO2) and lithium iron phosphate. It is preferred over lithium carbonate as a precursor for lithium nickel manganese cobalt oxides.


=== Grease ===
A popular lithium grease thickener is lithium 12-hydroxystearate, which produces a general-purpose lubricating grease due to its high resistance to water and usefulness at a range of temperatures.


=== Carbon dioxide scrubbing ===

Lithium hydroxide is used in breathing gas purification systems for spacecraft, submarines, and rebreathers to remove carbon dioxide from exhaled gas by producing lithium carbonate and water:

2 LiOH·H2O + CO2 → Li2CO3 + 3 H2O
or

2 LiOH + CO2 → Li2CO3 + H2O
The latter, anhydrous hydroxide, is preferred for its lower mass and lesser water production for respirator systems in spacecraft. One gram of anhydrous lithium hydroxide can remove 450 cm3 of carbon dioxide gas. The monohydrate loses its water at 100–110 °C.


=== Precursor ===
Lithium hydroxide, together with lithium carbonate, is a key intermediates used for the production of other lithium compounds, illustrated by its use in the production of lithium fluoride:

LiOH + HF → LiF + H2O


=== Other uses ===
It is also used in ceramics and some Portland cement formulations, where it is also used to suppress ASR (concrete cancer). 
Lithium hydroxide (isotopically enriched in lithium-7) is used to alkalize the reactor coolant in pressurized water reactors for corrosion control.
It is good radiation protection against free neutrons.

== Early life and education ==
Utemuratov was born in 1957. His father worked in the judiciary and was a prosecutor, and his mother worked as an accountant. Utemuratov graduated from the Faculty of Economics of the Almaty Institute of National Economy in 1981.

After Kazakhstan became an independent country in 1991, President Nursultan Nazarbayev selected Utemuratov to go to Vienna and find economic opportunities for the new republic. He helped broker Kazakhstan’s first line of credit with Bank Creditanstalt.
After Utemuratov returned, he was made first deputy minister for external economic affairs. In 1995, Utemuratov, jointly with a group of partners, founded the Almaty Trade and Finance (ATF) Bank; by 2007 he was its controlling shareholder. In 2007, Utemuratov, along with minority shareholders, sold ATF Bank to the Italian UniCredit group for $2.3 billion. As of 2007, this transaction was the largest in the Central Independent States banking sector. Subsequent reports suggested that Utemuratov's stake in ATF had been larger than previously known and that he and family members made more than $1 billion from the sale.
In 1999, Utemuratov was a member of the United Nations Economic Commission for Europe.
From 2003 to 2006 he was secretary of the security council and head of the presidential property management directorate from 2006 to 2008.
From 2008 to 2013, Utemuratov, was described as an "adviser to the president" and held a role as a special representative to Kyrgyzstan.
This close relationship with Nazarbayev led to Utemuratov being given the nickname "the grey cardinal" in Kazakhstan. The Times quoted a spokeswoman for Utemuratov saying he: "is neither a middleman, nor proxy for President Nazarbayev" and that he had not profited from his relationship with the President.
